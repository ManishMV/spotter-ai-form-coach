# How Spotter coaches — cue wording and feedback policy

The form rules ([squat](SQUAT-FORM.md), [push-up](PUSHUP-FORM.md)) decide what counts and what's a
fault. This doc covers **what Spotter says and when**, based on motor-learning research. Researched Nov 12, 2026.

## Terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **No-rep** | The rep didn't meet the standard, so it doesn't count; the reason is shown on that rep, every time | "Sit deeper", "Keep your feet side by side", "Slow down" |
| **Form cue** | The rep counted, but here's how to do it better. Comes in three weights: safety, technique and minor | "Keep your chest up", "Lower with control" |
| **Praise** | Positive feedback, naming the fix after a correction | "Nice depth", "Good rep" |
| **Position prompt** | Where to stand or what to show the camera | "Turn to your side", "Move back so your whole body is in view" |

The **Coaching** setting (All cues / Safety only / Off) controls form cues and praise only. No-reps and
position prompts are always on, because without them the count would look random.

## Cue wording

| Principle | What Spotter does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **External focus beats internal focus.** Cue the effect on the environment, not body parts. This helps performance and learning across ages and skill levels. | "Sit deeper" (chair image), "Lead with your chest" | Wulf & Lewthwaite 2021 (OPTIMAL theory); Chua et al. 2021 meta-analysis (g ≈ 0.26); Winkelman, *The Language of Coaching* |
| **Near targets for beginners.** Close, concrete targets suit novices better than distant ones. | Targets are the floor and an imagined chair | Singh & Wulf 2020 (cited in Wulf & Lewthwaite 2021) |
| **1–3 words, one cue at a time.** Fix the worst problem first. | One cue per rep, chosen by priority | Kushner et al. 2015 (NSCA); Benz/Winkelman (NSCA) |
| **Say what to do, not what to avoid.** | "Heels down", not "Don't lift your heels" | ACE cueing guidance |

## Feedback policy

| Rule | Spotter's setting | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Bandwidth:** only coach outside a tolerance | Each cue has a threshold; small deviations stay quiet | Sherwood 1988; Lee & Carnahan 1990 |
| **Weighted by risk** | **Safety** cues speak up on the **first** rep and may repeat every **2nd** rep while the fault continues. **Technique** cues wait for a pattern (**2 of the last 3** reps) and repeat at most every **3rd** rep. **Minor** cues only if it keeps happening (**3 of the last 4** reps), at most every **4th** rep. | Staying quiet costs more for injury risk than a rare false alarm. Pattern rule: Myer et al. 2014 (scores a fault at 2+ of 10 reps). No-nag rule: Wulf & Lewthwaite 2021 ("occasionally ignoring mistakes") |
| **Not too chatty** | At most one cue per rep, and a non-safety cue stays quiet if a cue was shown on the previous rep | Wulf & Lewthwaite 2021; product judgement to avoid irritation |
| **Praise good reps** | On the first clean rep after a cue, name the fix ("Nice depth"). Otherwise "Good rep" every **4th** clean rep. | Chiviacowsky & Wulf 2002, 2007; Badami 2012 |
| **Reps that don't count always say why** | "Keep your feet side by side", "Sit deeper", "Slow down"… | Information about the count, not coaching, so it's never held back |

## Cues by exercise (priority order)

| Exercise | Cue | Fault | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Squat | "Keep your chest up" | Torso leaning far more than the shins through the bottom of the squat | **Technique** | Loads the lower back (Myer 2014; Kushner 2015). Moved from safety on Nov 13: with body weight the load is low, and speaking on the first rep felt naggy |
| Squat | "Lead with your chest" | Hips rising before the chest | **Safety** | Shifts load to the lower back on the way up (Myer criterion 10; Kushner 2015) |
| Squat | "Lower with control" | Dropping into the bottom in under 0.5 s | Technique | One fast rep isn't dangerous; a habit of dropping is (Myer: descent slower than ascent) |
| Squat | "A bit deeper" | Thigh still above parallel (the rep counted) | Technique | Progress, not injury. Different words from the no-rep reason "Sit deeper", so a counted rep never sounds like a missed one |
| Push-up | "Hips up" | Hips sagging | **Safety** | Lower-back extension, the top safety fault (ACE; Army) |
| Push-up | "Stay off the floor" | Resting on the floor at the bottom for over 0.8 s | Minor | A quick chest-to-floor touch is good form; only resting counts |
| Push-up | "Lower with control" | Dropping to the bottom in under 0.4 s | Technique | Control on the way down |
| Push-up | "Hips down" | Hips piking | Technique | Makes the exercise easier, no back load (NASM) |

Reps that break a **counting** rule (lunge, half rep, knee push-up, big sag, bouncing, no lockout) are never
coached this way: they don't count, and the reason shows on that rep, every time.

## Voice and sound (Step 11)

Users usually stand 2–3 m from the device, so sound is the main channel.

| Event | What you hear |
|---|---|
| Tap Start | "Get ready" (also unlocks sound on phones) |
| Countdown | Starts only once you're set (in view, side-on, every needed body part visible, in the start position) for 1 s: "3, 2, 1, Go", a tick under 3-2-1 and a short start tone on "Go". Moving out of position stops it. Before that: nothing is spoken for the first 4 s after the camera comes on (time to prop up the device and step back); then one where-to-stand prompt at a time, spoken after 2 s on screen, at least 4 s apart, at most twice each |
| Rep counted | A rising chime. A cue only if needed ("Keep your chest up"), never bundled with the number. Rep numbers out loud are optional (*Count reps out loud*, off by default: the chime is enough) |
| Rep didn't count | A low double tone, plus the reason: "Sit deeper." |
| Where to stand | "Turn to your side", "Move back so your feet are in view" (names the part that is cut off: feet, knees or hands; otherwise "your whole body"): see the calm-start rules above |
| Praise | Spoken only when it names a fix ("Nice depth"); plain "Good rep" stays on screen |
| Near a rep target | "Three more / Two more / Last one", only while you rest for a few seconds, never attached to a rep |
| Set ends | Waits for the coach to finish, then the set-complete sound, then "Nice work" |

**Speech policy (one message at a time):** fast sets produce events faster than speech, so not everything
can be said. Without rules, cues cut each other off and come out jammed together (found when testing mixed-form
sets). The rules, like a real coach's:
1. **Ranked:** safety cue > reason a rep didn't count > technique cue > praise > rep number. A message may only
   cut off something less important than itself.
2. **Breathing room:** after the coach speaks, technique cues and praise wait about 3 s, reasons about 1.5 s,
   safety cues about 1.2 s. Anything held back still shows on screen.
3. **Nothing said late:** a message that has to wait is dropped if it can't start within about 1 s.
4. **One short line per rep:** a cue or the number, never bundled ("Keep your chest up", not "Four. Chest up. Two more").
   Numbers only into silence, and off by default; the chime marks every rep.
5. **Target countdown only while resting,** so it can't clash with coaching during fast reps.

Tested with a simulated clock on mixed-form and fast, sloppy sets: no cut-offs, and lines start at least
1.6 s apart.

**Natural voice (ElevenLabs):** every line is a pre-recorded clip, generated once and shipped with the app,
so there are no API calls during a workout. Four voices, chosen by ear in the Test lab: **Arjun** (Indian
English, male), **Meera** (Indian English, female), **Max** (American English, male) and **Ava** (American
English, female). Clips are made with Eleven v4 plus a tone hint per kind of line (cues *firm and
encouraging*, praise *warm*, reasons *calm*, position prompts *friendly*). Four delivery styles were
compared by ear, and this one was the most natural. The silence ElevenLabs adds around each clip is trimmed,
so the pause between number and cue is exactly 300 ms. Any line without a clip falls back to browser speech.
Regenerate with `npm run voice:generate` (styles in `scripts/delivery.ts`).

**Sounds:** ElevenLabs sound effects were compared against simple built-in tones for each sound. Only the
countdown tick (a single crisp click) was kept from ElevenLabs. For short, frequent feedback (rep counted,
rep didn't count, Go, set complete), the built-in tones were shorter, less tiring over a long set and sounded
like one family. All sounds are levelled to the same loudness. Regenerate options with `npm run sounds:generate`.

## Considered, not in v1
- **Push-up head position** (NASM: forward head; Myer: neck in line with the torso). MediaPipe's ear points are too noisy for a live cue. Planned for v1.1 as an after-set tip.
- **A user setting for cue amount** (all / errors only / summary): research shows little benefit (McKay et al. 2023 meta-analysis, preprint). It's a later nice-to-have.
